# Check upstream receptiveness before recommending a contribution

Before suggesting an upstream contribution, inspect the repository's README, CONTRIBUTING guidance,
issue and PR templates, and relevant maintainer statements where available. Check whether outside work
of this kind is welcome, including any restrictions on AI-assisted contributions. Repository activity
alone does not establish that maintainers accept outside contributions.

Look at recent outside contributions comparable in scope and platform. Include merged, closed-unmerged,
and still-open PRs; a sample of successful merges alone hides the backlog. Distinguish human outside
contributors from maintainers and bots, and separate the first substantive maintainer response from
merge or closure. Check whether a stalled PR is waiting on its author before attributing the delay to
maintainers. Keep the investigation proportionate to the proposed work.

Use about 15 calendar days as a rough benchmark for an initial substantive maintainer response.
Assess merge time separately, accounting for scope, review exchanges, and documented maintainer
availability. Treat this as a judgment aid; a slower project may still be worth contributing to.

Before recommending the investment, summarize the evidence with dated links and any important limits
on the sample. If acceptance or turnaround is uncertain, say so and explain the consequences for the
user's immediate problem and local maintenance. Let the user decide whether the expected benefit
justifies the contribution effort and possible wait.

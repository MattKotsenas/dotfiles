# Editing

Check the draft against the prose baseline, then make the following pass. These criteria also apply when
reviewing someone else's prose. Examples of unwanted wording illustrate problems; they are not suggested text.

## Structure and emphasis

Give each fact one authoritative home. Change that home when the fact changes; elsewhere, reference it rather
than restating it or appending an "updated" note. Remove repeated explanations and distinctive phrases reused
as callbacks.

Put the result or behavior the reader needs first. Keep supporting steps and qualifications close enough to
prevent a misleading impression. Make the ending useful: finish the explanation or identify the next action.
Remove introductions that narrate the writing, conclusions that merely repeat it, and body text that echoes a
heading without adding information. Keep cutting until a further cut would lose meaning or make the text
harder to follow.

Cut defensive caveats answering objections nobody raised. Keep qualifications that affect correctness or the
reader's decision. Compare viable alternatives in their strongest reasonable form.

Use consistent terminology and established technical names. Explain unfamiliar terms and their role. Do not
invent labels such as "the configuration trap" or "port drift" and present them as established concepts.

## Sentences

Prefer verbs to nominal phrases: "decide" instead of "make a decision". Prefer active voice when it makes the
actor and action clear. Address the reader as "you" when giving instructions and use the imperative:
"Create the resource" instead of "You should create the resource".

Split a sentence when its qualifications or nested asides require rereading. Keep useful technical parentheses;
remove those used for dramatic pauses or avoidable digressions.

Watch for rhetorical corrective contrast such as "It's not X, it's Y", "The question isn't X", and "Not X.
Not Y. Just Z." Apply the baseline's prohibition by stating the point directly.

Rewrite self-answered questions such as "The result? Faster startup." Write "Startup is faster." Replace
fragments used for emphasis, such as "Local. Fast. Free.", with complete sentences. Vary sentence structure
without manufacturing drama.

Remove trailing clauses that claim significance without explaining anything, such as ", highlighting its
flexibility", and short phrases hung off a final comma. Replace "serves as", "stands as", or "represents" with
"is" when the meaning is the same. Use "from X to Y" only when X and Y are ends of a real range.

## Words and rhetorical habits

Use this vocabulary blocklist when editing newly authored prose. Preserve exact quotations, identifiers, and
literal technical meanings; do not corrupt source material to remove a word.

- Marketing language: seamless, unlock, elevate, supercharge, game-changing, cutting-edge, unprecedented.
- Empty intensifiers: quietly, deeply, fundamentally, remarkably, arguably, truly.
- Stock AI vocabulary: delve, harness, utilize, leverage as a verb, streamline, certainly, tapestry, landscape,
  paradigm, synergy, load-bearing, and "gated" meaning restricted. Use "use" instead of "utilize" or "leverage".
  Use "ecosystem" and "framework" only in their literal technical senses.

Describe the action and consequence instead of inflating stakes or making an empty quotable claim.

Cut filler transitions such as "It's worth noting", "Importantly", "Interestingly", "Notably", "Here's the
thing", and "Here's the catch". Remove performative candor such as "And yes" and "To be honest". Say where
something is defined, stored, or configured instead of "where it actually lives".

Do not claim familiarity or authority with "famously", "notoriously", "a classic", or "as you know". Replace
unnamed appeals such as "experts recommend" or "many teams find" with a specific source or remove the claim.
Do not list historical companies or technology shifts to manufacture authority.

Use an analogy only when it explains the subject more clearly than a direct account. Avoid speculative
openings such as "Imagine a world where".

## Lists and formatting

Use tables for useful comparisons or lookup. Remove reflexive groups of three and counts announced before a
list. A list should have the items the subject needs.
Keep grammar and punctuation parallel: complete sentences take periods; fragments do not.

Do not turn every bullet into a bold-led miniature section. Reserve repeated bold term labels for definitions
where that format helps lookup. Keep headings specific and informative, using repository conventions for case.

The baseline's bans include validation openers such as "Great question" and "You're absolutely right",
conversational back-references such as "Yes, as we discussed", restatement filler such as "In other words" and
"Simply put", and scaffolding such as "One important thing to note is". Delete them rather than substituting
another empty lead-in.
